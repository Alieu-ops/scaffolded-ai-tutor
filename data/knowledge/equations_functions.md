# Equations and functions in business (draft notes, written with AI assistance, under my review)

## Solving linear equations
Do the same operation to both sides until the unknown is alone. Collect the unknown terms on one side and the numbers on the other. Remove brackets first by multiplying out, or divide both sides by the number outside the bracket. Check the answer by substituting it back into the original equation. Common mistake: changing the sign of a term on one side only, or forgetting to multiply every term inside a bracket.

## Rearranging formulae
To make a letter the subject, undo the operations around it in reverse order, doing the same to both sides. Example: from A = P + Prt, factorise P out: A = P(1 + rt), so P = A / (1 + rt). Common mistake: dividing only one term of a sum.

## Cost, revenue and profit
Total cost TC = fixed cost + variable cost per unit x quantity. Total revenue TR = price x quantity. Profit = TR - TC. Fixed costs (rent, salaries) do not change with output; variable costs (materials) rise with each unit. Common mistake: forgetting to include fixed costs in total cost.

## Break-even analysis
Break-even is the output where TR = TC, so profit is zero. Contribution per unit = price - variable cost per unit. Break-even quantity = fixed costs / contribution per unit. Above break-even the firm makes a profit; below it, a loss. The answer is usually rounded UP to a whole unit if units cannot be split.

## Supply, demand and market equilibrium
A demand function shows quantity demanded falling as price rises (e.g. Qd = a - bP); a supply function shows quantity supplied rising with price (e.g. Qs = c + dP). At equilibrium, Qd = Qs. Set the two expressions equal, solve for P, then substitute P into either function to find Q. Check that both functions give the same Q.

## Quadratic functions
A quadratic has the form y = ax^2 + bx + c. If a is negative the graph is an upside-down U with a maximum; if a is positive it is a U with a minimum. The turning point is at x = -b / (2a). Roots (where y = 0) can be found by factorising, or with the formula x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a). Tip: divide every term by a common factor first to make factorising easier. In business, the roots of a profit function are the break-even outputs.
