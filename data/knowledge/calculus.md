## Differentiation rules
The derivative dy/dx measures the rate of change of y as x changes (the slope of the graph). Power rule: the derivative of ax^n is anx^(n-1). The derivative of a constant is 0; the derivative of ax is a. Differentiate a sum term by term. To find the derivative at a point, differentiate first, then substitute the value of x. Common mistake: substituting the value of x before differentiating.

## Product and quotient rules
Product rule: if y = uv, then dy/dx = u(dv/dx) + v(du/dx). Quotient rule: if y = u/v, then dy/dx = (v(du/dx) - u(dv/dx)) / v^2. Alternative for a product of two polynomials: multiply out the brackets first, then use the power rule.

## Marginal cost, revenue and profit
Marginal cost MC is the derivative of total cost with respect to quantity: MC = d(TC)/dq. It is approximately the cost of producing one more unit. Marginal revenue MR = d(TR)/dq. If the demand function gives price P in terms of q, first form total revenue TR = P x q, then differentiate. Marginal profit = MR - MC. Fixed costs disappear when differentiating, so they do not affect marginal cost.

## Maximum and minimum values
At a maximum or minimum (a stationary point) the derivative is zero. Steps: (1) differentiate; (2) set the derivative equal to 0 and solve; (3) use the second derivative to decide: negative means a maximum, positive means a minimum; (4) substitute the value back into the ORIGINAL function to find the maximum or minimum value. Profit is maximised where marginal revenue equals marginal cost (MR = MC). Common mistake: giving the output q when the question asks for the maximum profit itself.
